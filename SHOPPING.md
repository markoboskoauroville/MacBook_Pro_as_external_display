# SHOPPING LIST

Written 21.10.2026. Prices come from a web search, not a live check: look at each product's own
page on amazon.de before you buy.

**The two Macs:**
- **Screen (receiver):** this Mac, the MacBook Pro 14-inch M1 Pro (MacBookPro18,3), panel
  3024 × 1964. The area under the notch is 3024 × 1890, which is 16:10. The BenQ stays on it.
- **Sender:** the MacBook Pro 16-inch M1 Pro (2021). An M1 Pro drives two external screens.
  Its HDMI port is free for the Nano, and the dummy plug goes on a USB-C port.

| # | What | Must have | Found (not checked live) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Dummy display plug, DisplayPort** | the EDID table must list **2560 × 1600 @ 60 Hz** (16:10) | FUERAN DisplayPort emulator, 2560 × 1600 @ 60 and 4K @ 60 |
| 2 | **USB-C to DisplayPort adapter** (for #1) | DP 1.4, 4K60 | any brand, about 10 € |
| 3 | **Thunderbolt 4 cable, 0.8 m** | Intel-certified, lightning mark, 40 Gbps (it is not FireWire, and a charging cable will not work) | Maxonar TB4 0.8 m (about 6–15 €), UGREEN US501 0.8 m, Cable Matters 0.8 m, Anker 0.7 m (39.99 €) |
| – | for step 1 (Nano) | the 16-inch's own HDMI port → the Nano with the HDMI cable he already uses for it | nothing to buy |

Why DisplayPort and not HDMI: most cheap HDMI dummy plugs offer only 16:9 modes (1080p, 1440p,
4K), and 16:9 leaves black bars on this 16:10 screen. The DisplayPort emulators list 2560 × 1600.
An HDMI dummy is fine **only if** its EDID table shows 2560 × 1600.

2560 × 1600 is shown at 3024 × 1890, which is 1.18 times larger and a little soft. On the day we
try BetterDisplay to make the dummy an exact 3024 × 1890, or 1512 × 945 HiDPI.

**If nothing is bought:** BetterDisplay's free virtual screen at any size (0 €) can stand in for
#1 and #2 for the first tests. The cable is still needed.
